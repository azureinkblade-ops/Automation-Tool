# Hermes Authority Model

## 1. Purpose

This document defines who may review, decide, authorize, execute, write, and promote information in Hermes.

## 2. Authority classes

### Human owner

Highest decision authority within the local deployment.

May:

- accept or reject ADRs;
- issue execution authorizations;
- amend governance policy;
- resolve escalations;
- revoke agents or models;
- approve high-risk memory promotion.

May not bypass audit recording through the supported system interface.

### Hermes governance engine

May:

- validate policy;
- calculate deterministic consensus;
- change authoritative workflow state;
- issue machine-generated acceptance artifacts;
- validate execution authorization;
- reject scope violations;
- maintain the audit ledger.

May not fabricate human approval.

### Deterministic validator

May:

- read declared inputs;
- calculate hashes;
- validate schemas;
- verify identities and constraints;
- produce deterministic reports.

May not interpret ambiguous policy or authorize execution.

### Reviewer model

May:

- read an approved evidence snapshot;
- identify findings;
- cite evidence;
- classify severity;
- report uncertainty;
- recommend a disposition.

May not:

- execute commands;
- edit evidence;
- approve a gate;
- sign for a human;
- modify governance state;
- repair its own finding.

### Consensus engine

May:

- validate reviewer reports;
- normalize findings;
- apply a frozen policy;
- produce `ACCEPTED`, `ESCALATED`, `BLOCKED`, or `INCONCLUSIVE`.

May not execute the next phase.

### LibreChat

May:

- collect user requests;
- display status;
- present agents and reports;
- submit confirmation responses.

May not directly alter Hermes state.

### Obsidian

May hold canonical human-readable knowledge and accepted documentation.

It may not serve as a runtime authorization store.

### Anytype

May display structured operational projections and collect low-risk human annotations.

It may not directly set accepted or authorized status.

### Ollama

May serve registered models.

It has no governance authority.

### Open Interpreter

May execute operations explicitly listed in a validated envelope.

It may not select its own scope or approve its output.

## 4. Approval categories

### Policy-derived acceptance

Generated by Hermes when deterministic consensus conditions are satisfied.

### Human confirmation

A user action recorded with identity, scope, timestamp, and parent artifact hashes.

### Execution authorization

A bounded permission object for specified operations.

### Emergency override

A separately defined process that must record:

- reason;
- operator;
- affected policy;
- duration;
- scope;
- compensating controls;
- post-action review requirement.

An emergency override must not be represented as an ordinary reviewer approval.

## 5. Escalation rules

Escalate when:

- reviewers materially disagree;
- evidence is incomplete;
- policy is ambiguous;
- a high-risk finding remains unresolved;
- a model or tool identity changed;
- a requested operation exceeds authorization;
- canonical knowledge conflicts with runtime evidence.

## 6. Prohibited authority combinations

The following combinations are prohibited:

- reviewer plus execution worker in the same session;
- evidence generator plus sole approver;
- Anytype status edit plus direct Hermes gate mutation;
- LibreChat message plus automatic unrestricted execution;
- model-generated signature plus human-approval claim;
- mutable evidence plus acceptance without re-review.
